Lunar Site Navigator is a free single-file web tool comparing landing sites and dates near the lunar south pole. For any hour from November 2026 to October 2045, it shows the Sun and Earth against the local terrain horizon, the time until the next change, a power estimate, and a delay planner.

It uses NASA data, with no calibration: SPICE ephemerides (DE440) and LOLA elevation. Over 121 Site04 cells, Sun shows bias −0.3 points and MAE 1.3 against LROC camera maps. Against NASA PGDA, a separate LOLA-based calculation, MAE is 1.6 for Sun and 3.1 for Earth. At our 10 candidates the model overestimates Sun by about 7 points against LROC and 3 against PGDA.

Those maps measure how much, not when. For timing, a pre-fixed test compared the model with 5 dated LROC images: mean MCC 0.74, beating three controls. Limits: 5 dates in 2009, only Site04; it does not validate the 2026–2045 prediction; the model marks more lit points than the images; one frame stayed below 0.5.

Site 456, our main site, keeps Sun at 20–22 % when moved ±100 m, in a 4.47 km² pocket. Site 14, the contrast, has more light (49 % Sun) but swings across 33–58 %, in a 0.33 km² pocket, and is not confirmed at pixel scale.

Line of sight is not a communications link. Candidates are our own analysis, not NASA selections. Nine points in NASA-named regions stay unranked. Renders are illustrative and AI-assisted. It compares; it does not certify.
